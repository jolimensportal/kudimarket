One trade, seen from both sides.

KudiMarket holds a board with a cedi buyer on one side and a naira seller on the other, and sits in the middle of it as a counterparty in its own right. This is what that looks like on a phone: the seller's side, the buyer's side, and the record both of them end up trusting.

Every figure here is a working figure out of the ledger, not filler. Amounts, rates and the state changes come out of the same tables the operations console reads.

The seller

Ama has naira. She needs cedi, not naira.

She is not a company and does not want to be one. She lists what she holds, sets the rate she will accept, and waits. KudiMarket sits in the middle of the board at a rate we can honour ourselves, which is why she never has to find a counterparty before she can trade.

The buyer

Ama has cedi. She needs naira, sent to Lagos.

She came for one number and left knowing who is standing behind it. That is the whole product: not a better rate, but a rate with a name on it.

What both sides end up with

A record that cannot be edited afterwards.

This is the same trade after a dispute, on both phones. Neither side wrote it, and neither side can change it. Every line commits to the one above it, so a row that was edited or quietly deleted is detectable by anyone who asks. The same is true of our own prices, which is the other thing a trader is entitled to check: every rate we publish is on file forever, chained, and never edited in place.
