## 1. The name, and why it works

**Kudi** — Hausa for money. Hausa and Dagbani are both Niger-Congo languages, so the word travels into Ghana’s northern trade belt where Dagbani is spoken.
**Market** — the English word for *ɔdɔn* / *fia* in Twi and Ga, and for the open-air market generally.

Ghana note: *kudi* is strongest in the north, where Dagbani is spoken and Hausa is
used in trade. Accra and Kumasi will carry the name on English and the escrow
promise, not on the Hausa. That is acceptable — the name travels, the meaning is
earned by the product.

Two languages in one word. That is not a coincidence to work around; it is the
brand. The customer is someone who moves between languages daily — who reads a
price in the local language, sends a message in English, and closes both deals.

### The recall mechanics

The whole naming test, in four properties:

| Property | How KudiMarket satisfies it |
|---|---|
| Two chunks | `KU-di` / `MAR-ket` — say it once, spell it right |
| No expiry trigger | Zero crypto vocabulary. No coin, bit, chain, token, exchange |
| Pre-loaded image | Everyone who hears it already sees a market |
| Scope headroom | Works for cedi today, cedi tomorrow, without a rebrand |

Compare the names that did not survive: **Paxful** (soft consonants, no image),
**Patricia** (a person's name carrying no meaning), **LocalBitcoins** (locked to
two now-awkward words), **Binance** (binary finance — clever, but it aged into
a synonym for complexity). Each one had a word that was a liability.

## 2. Positioning

> For traders in West African markets who need to move between cedi and crypto
> without learning a system, KudiMarket is the exchange that feels like the
> market itself — because it is one. Not a terminal, not a casino, not a bank.

**The enemy is not a competitor. It is a feeling.** Patricia taught the market
what it feels like when an app freezes your money. Binance taught it what it
feels like when an app has too many buttons. KudiMarket's entire job is to feel
obviously different from both.

### Category frame

| | Binance | Paxful / LocalBitcoins | **KudiMarket** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feels like | A terminal | A classifieds board | **A market** |
| Learns | A lot | A lot, about safety | **Nothing** |
| Trust from | Scale and liquidity | Listings and history | **Escrow you can see** |
| Weakness | Complexity | No escrow layer | Must nail trust on day one |

## 3. Tagline

# Your market. Your rate.

Restored 2026-10-06 by user decision. A replacement ("Know before it moves.") was
developed, scored against 15 candidates and briefly adopted. The objections to it
are on record in `tagline.html` and are **accepted, not resolved**:

- Two-part symmetric construction — the most recognisable machine-written shape.
- Abstract: you cannot picture "your market" or "your rate".
- Unfalsifiable: everyone's market is their market.
- Swap-any-competitor test: works with Binance substituted, so it claims no advantage.

**Why it was kept anyway:** it is the only line that carries both halves of the
position — the market (P2P, people, listings) and the rate (control, no slippage,
your price not theirs). A visitor who arrives cold understands KudiMarket in one
glance. `brand/tagline.html` is kept as the record of the alternative and of the
trade-off, not as a pending change.


### Support lines (rotating, not competing)

- Escrow you can see.
- Nobody touches your money until you say so.
- The rate you agreed is the rate you get.
- Buy, sell, settle. Nothing else.
- From market to pocket in a minute.

## 4. Voice

Market speech, not fintech speech. Short sentences. Concrete nouns. No
adjectives doing work that a fact could do.

**We say / we don't say**

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "Escrow holds your cedi until you confirm." | "Bank-grade security infrastructure." |
| "You set the price." | "Our best prices, always." |
| "It didn't work. Here's what happened." | "Something went wrong. Try again." |
| "Call us." | "Reach out to our 24/7 support team." |

**Rules**

1. Never use a crypto buzzword as decoration. If the word is not load-bearing, cut it.
2. Always name the actor. "Escrow holds the money" beats "funds are secured."
3. Admit limits out loud before the user finds them.
4. One idea per screen. The simplicity claim has to be kept, not just made.
5. Hausa may appear as warmth, never as translation debt — if a Ghanaian trader cannot get it in one tap, it failed. Northern Ghana is the stronghold; Accra and Kumasi need the English to carry the meaning.

### Market correction — Ghana, not Nigeria

The first pass of this brand was built for Nigeria and every figure in it was
measured against Nigerian institutions. Corrected 6 Oct 2026:

- Currency **₵ cedi** (GHS), minor unit pesewa. Was naira.
- Competitor field re-measured against **Ghanaian** institutions — GCB, Ecobank,
  Stanbic, Consolidated, Fidelity, First Bank, and local fintechs Paystack,
  Zeepay, Chipper, Expresspay, Hubtel.
- **Plum re-checked against Ghana: nearest is Zeepay at 37.61 units.** No
  Ghanaian institution is close. The "does not look like a bank" property holds
  in the right country.
- Cultural frame rebuilt on **kente, Adinkra and the Ashanti goldweights** —
  which is a better fit than what it replaced. Plum is a literal kente colour,
  and the Ashanti goldweights are precision scales, which is exactly what an
  escrow does.
- The crypto half of the field is unchanged: Binance, KuCoin, CoinGate and Paxful
  are global brands, so those measurements carried over.

`kudilint`'s ban list was rewritten to hold Ghanaian institutions. Nigerian banks
were removed because protecting against looking like a Nigerian bank is not a
requirement for a Ghanaian launch.

### The three-state law

```
PLUM  = PENDING / ACTION      A choice, not a verdict
GREEN = CONFIRMATION ONLY     Never a button. Ever.
RED   = LOSS / DESTRUCTIVE    Cancel, decline, failed, closed
```

**A colour has exactly one job. Two jobs kills it.**

Green is a confirmation *panel*, never a CTA, because every green that holds white
text sits 0.16–0.67 OKLab units from a Ghanaian bank (`#214D2C` is 0.16 from
Bitstamp, 0.57 from Access). As a panel it reads as system feedback; as a button it
would read as a bank.

### Why plum, and why this plum

Brand hue is **330** at chroma 0.095 — 30 degrees clear of magenta, which is what
made the previous hue-352 plum read pink (the rejected hex is listed in
`kudilint/kudi.config.json`). Clearance to the nearest of 38 measured brands is
**48.5 units**.

The hue cannot rotate toward red: 0–30 is the loss band, and a brand there would
share a family with the danger colour. It cannot rotate much further toward violet
either, because CoinGate owns bright lavender. **Hue 330 is the ceiling.** If it ever
reads too purple, go *darker at the same hue*, never a bigger rotation.

### Four non-negotiable rules

1. **The data rule.** Plum is never on a number. Prices, balances and rates are
   always ink on bone. The brand colour is the frame; the data is never dressed in it.
2. **Never both.** Plum and red in one button group means two warnings at once.
   Destructive actions sit in their own row, below a divider, or behind a confirm step.
3. **Confirm in words.** Name the amount and the consequence: "Cancel this trade and
   return ₵1,742,000 to your balance?" Never a bare "Are you sure?"
4. **Sell and Buy are never split by colour.** Both actions use the same treatment.
   The price and the confirmation screen carry the distinction, in words and cedi.
   Green is never a button.

**Accessibility verified.** Every text pairing above passes WCAG AA or better.
`Plum Light` (4.70:1) and `Loss Fill` (4.30:1) are restricted to large text,
fills and indicators. Nothing a user must read is ever set in either.

**Outstanding check.** Confirm no Ghanaian bank occupies this plum. No competitor
was found in the hue band, but it was never formally checked.

### Typography

One family, weight doing the work. Not the platform defaults and not the 2020-23
trend grotesques; the banned list is enforced in `kudilint/kudi.config.json`.

- **Wordmark:** Instrument Sans — *kudi* light in Plum Deep, *market* 800 in Plum Market (see `darker.html`)
- **UI:** Instrument Sans at 14px minimum, tabular figures on every currency value
- **Numerals:** tabular lining figures everywhere — this is a money product and rates must align in columns

### Wordmark directions — tested 2026-10-06, direction 1c chosen

> Historical record. These were the three typographic directions evaluated. **1c was
> chosen and its typeface was subsequently replaced** — the display grotesk was
> rejected as AI-default slop and swapped for Instrument Sans. The table is kept
> because it records what was tried, not because it is the current state.

| Id | Concept | Type | Character | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1c** | Two Languages, One Word | Bricolage light + 800, stacked | **Recommended.** Makes the bilingual name structural | Ten characters is long; the stacked lockup fixes it |
| **1b** | The Weighed Word | Bricolage 800 condensed, caps, balance beam | Most authoritative; institutional trust | Reads louder and colder than "simple" |
| **1a** | The Tray | Familjen Grotesk 500 + 700, woven texture | Most human; argues the warmth case | Texture can read dated if overdone |

Open `index.html` to compare all three. 1c won on concept — the light/heavy
split *is* the signature — and that concept survived the typeface change. The
current rendering is in `darker.html`.

**Mark:** not yet produced. The three files contain labelled placeholder zones

commission a designer for the actual mark. Brief: a *kudi* pot silhouette, or a
woven-tray arc. It must read at 16px.

## 6. Naming architecture

One word, one product, no coin of your own until there is a reason.

| Layer | Rule |
|---|---|
| Master brand | **KudiMarket** — never rebranded, never an acronym |
| Product line | Plain English sub-names: *Instant*, *P2P*, *Escrow*. No numbered suffix |
| Own token, if ever | Prefix `KUDI-`. Never rebrand the parent for it |
| Support | `@kudimarket` handles; never a number-heavy handle |

**Never** launch `KudiMarket Pro`, `KudiX`, `Kudi 2.0`, or a parent rebrand when a
new asset appears. The parent name is the asset — it has to survive every
generation the business grows into.

## 8. Before you print anything

**Blocking — must happen before launch spend:**

1. **NIPO trademark search** for KudiMarket in the relevant Nice classes,
   via an accredited agent. A CAC company-name search does **not** cover
   trademarks — verified against published Ghanaian IP due-diligence guidance,
   which is explicit on this point. Expect a 3–5 business day turnaround.
2. **CAC name reservation** — separate process, and passing it grants no
   trademark rights.
3. **Verify the "Kudi fintech" conflict.** A Kudi-branded fintech presence in
   Ghana was flagged during naming but could not be confirmed; search
   rate-limited. Check it directly. If a live Kudi brand holds class 36/42,
   the compound may still be distinct — but know before you commit.
4. **Register defensively:** kudimarket.com (primary), kudimarkets.com,
   kudi-market.com, and the .ng equivalents once you have a Ghanaian
   presence. `.ng` registry WHOIS timed out and could not be verified here.

**Non-blocking but real:**

5. Linguistic read-through by native Dagbani and Ghanaian English speakers, plus
   a Twi speaker. Confirm "kudi" reads as money — not as a person's name, not as
   anything unintended — in Accra, Kumasi and Tamale.
6. Confirm the name is not awkward for Ghanaian (Tw/i), Kenyan (Swahili) and
   Anglophone West African expansion before scaling spend.
