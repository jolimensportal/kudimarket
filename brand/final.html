Read the two screens together. On the left, pine and green agree — money moving, funds held. On the right, oxblood takes over completely and pine disappears from every action. The user never has to work out which colour is the brand and which is the alarm: alarm colours are never the brand colour.
The destructive rule
Oxblood appears only where the user loses something or ends something. Cancel, decline, close, delete, dispute, withdraw, failed login. If it is on the screen, it is a warning.
Never both
Oxblood and pine in the same button group means two warnings at once. Destructive actions are always visually separated — their own row, a divider above, or a confirm step. Never side by side with a primary action.
Confirm step
Destructive actions get a confirmation that names the amount and the consequence in words. "Cancel this trade and return ₵1,742,000 to your balance?" — not a bare Are you sure?
The data rule holds
Pine is never on a number. Prices, balances and rates are always ink on bone. That rule was doing the heavy lifting under oxblood; it still does it here.
Why this beats oxblood-as-brand
Under oxblood-as-brand, red meant both brand and loss — two jobs, one hue. Now red means only stop and green means only safe. Every colour has exactly one job.
