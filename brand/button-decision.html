The rule
Oxblood owns every action. Bone is the button. Green never touches an action. One primary action colour, no second voice.
Green's only job
Green means exactly one thing: your money is accounted for — escrow held, trade settled, funds released. It is a status signal, never a button. That single meaning is why it stays legible; green-as-button dies the day it means two things.
Why bone wins
Contrast goes up, not down: 11.9:1 against 5.01:1. It is colour-blind safe because a light button on a dark ground is separated by lightness, not hue — hue is exactly what these simulations destroy. And it makes every action look like one action.
Where green can appear
Status rows, the escrow banner, a settled check, a settled rate. Anywhere it means "safe." Nowhere near a price, a button, or a buy/sell label.
If you want a filled button
Use oxblood #822B2A on bone #FCF6EE — 8.40:1, largest contrast of any combination measured, and still one hue. Reserve #C1514D for hover only; at 2.66:1 it fails as text.
Sell vs Buy
Never split them by colour. That is the Binance/Bloomberg convention that makes people afraid of trading apps. Both buttons are bone. The price and the confirmation screen carry the distinction, in words and cedi figures.
Contrast ratios computed in WCAG relative luminance. Simulations use Machado et al. 2009 severity-1.0 matrices. Bone-on-oxblood and bone-on-#822B2A were re-measured after simulation; both remain above 7:1 for all three deficiency types.
