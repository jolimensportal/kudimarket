The previous plum sat at hue 352 — that is 8 degrees from pure magenta. That is the pink. And its lightness was 0.52, the lightest step in our own scale. Light plus magenta reads candy. Drop the lightness and rotate the hue away from red, and it becomes a plum.

30 degrees clear of pink. Darker by 0.14. Reads as plum, and the chroma is still high enough to be saturated.

One constraint holds. The hue cannot rotate toward red — 0–30 is the loss band, reserved for failure states, or the brand would sit in the same family as the danger colour. That closes off the warm direction entirely, so the fix is downward and toward violet only.

Tagline restored. You kept "Your market. Your rate." and I am not re-opening it. The objections stay on record in brand/tagline.html and in BRAND.md §3, marked accepted, not resolved — so the trade-off is documented rather than hidden, and nobody re-litigates it in six months. It is in the lockup above and back across all 11 brand files.
What darkening costs and buys. The lighter steps of the old scale were doing real work — a visible hover state and a large type colour. At L 0.38 the top of the scale is Plum Light at 4.70:1, which is AA at body size but not AAA. That is the honest cost of "darker": the palette compresses at the top, so hierarchy leans on weight and space instead of lightness. That is a legitimate trade, and it is the same discipline the Ink option needed.
One thing I did not change. Going further toward violet (say hue 315) starts approaching CoinGate's territory and would cost the isolation that justified this colour in the first place. Hue 330 is the furthest point you can rotate before paying for it. If C still reads too purple, the answer is option D — darker at the same hue — not a bigger rotation.
