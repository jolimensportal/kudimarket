A · Ink

#1C1916 · achromatic
  • ✓Wins at 16px and in one-colour print. Zero ambiguity, ever.
  • ✓Unaffected by low-gamut LCDs. Black is black on any panel.
  • ✓Cannot be mistaken for a bank, and cannot date.
  • ✗No warmth. On a painted sign it reads austere rather than local.
  • ✗Maximum restraint is the easiest thing for a competitor to match.
  • ✗Sits near Carbon, Slypes, OKX and Chipper Cash in the dark-neutral band.

C · Aubergine

#7C5666 · deep pigment
  • ✓Wins on a painted sign. Pigment has history; black does not.
  • ✓70.44 units from the nearest of 38 brands. Genuinely unoccupied.
  • ✓Warm in a market at dusk — matches how the product is actually used.
  • ✗Low chroma means it greys out slightly on washed-out budget panels.
  • ✗Needs a dark wordmark step; mid-tone alone is 6.21:1, not AAA.
  • ✗Can read composed rather than urgent at the moment of a trade.
Where Ink actually wins. Pure function. Favicons, one-colour print, cheap screens, and any surface where you need the brand to disappear and the numbers to carry. If KudiMarket ever ships a terminal, a receipt, or a POS integration, ink is the safer choice.
Where Aubergine actually wins. Distinctiveness, and warmth. You have now told me twice that generic work is uninteresting to you — you rejected clay, rejected pine, and rejected Bricolage for exactly this reason. Ink is the most defensible option and also the most predictable one. Aubergine is the only one of the three with zero competitors in its hue band and it is the only one that looks like it came from somewhere.
